# The operating manual

Systems, structures, procedures, standards and the money — for a one-person
studio in Ghana, built to run without inventing anything.

This is the document that answers **what, why, when, how, which, where, who and
whom** for every part of the business. It is not motivational. It is the thing you
open on a Monday morning when you cannot remember what you decided.

Researched **2026-11-06**. Marked **FACT** (sourced), **INFERENCE** (my reasoning),
**DECIDED** (already chosen), **PROPOSED** (not yet chosen).

> **Not professional advice.** The tax, registration and legal sections are
> researched from published sources and marked with them. Confirm anything with a
> Ghanaian accountant before acting. I am not one and this document does not
> pretend otherwise.

## 2. Foundations: what business is this?

### 2.1 The business, stated plainly

**SoftFire sells a written decision.** Not a website — the website is the
deliverable. What the client is buying is someone to end the ambiguity: *what will
this cost, what will it do, when will it be finished, and who do I call.*

**FACT:** the market it sells into is crowded at the bottom — five studios publish
entry prices of GH₵1,000–3,500 for templates delivered in 1–2 weeks. Everybody
sells the same thing. **INFERENCE:** the only durable difference is the one they
cannot copy without giving up their "from" price — a number that does not move.

### 2.2 The five fundamentals, in order

Order matters. When two conflict, the higher one wins.

| # | Fundamental | What it means when it costs you money |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Never write a number you cannot hold** | turn down the job rather than quote low |
| 2 | **The client owns the keys** | lose the hosting margin; keep the relationship |
| 3 | **Say what is not included, first** | fewer sales, no arguments |
| 4 | **Only claim what you measured** | slower launch |
| 5 | **One named person answers** | you cannot scale by hiring |

**INFERENCE:** number 4 is the one that will feel excessive and will turn out to
be the most valuable. It is why this business has a 89-check verifier and a brand
document that says UNKNOWN seventeen times.

## 3. Structure: the org chart of one person

A sole trader has no hierarchy, so the failure mode is not politics — it is
**role collapse**. The same person does sales, delivery, accounts and support, and
the one that is least urgent (accounts) is the one that never happens.

**The fix is not a team. It is separate hats with separate days.**

| Role | What only this hat does | When | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seller** | replies to enquiries, writes quotes, says no | first hour of the day | 45 min/day |
| **Builder** | build, test, deploy — no messages | the middle block, phone off | 4 h/day |
| **Support** | answers client changes, in the plan or quoted | after the build block | 45 min/day |
| **Operator** | books, files, backups, records, the check-in | Friday | 2 h/week |
| **Accountant** | invoices, receipts, tax set-aside, monthly close | end of month | 2 h/month |
| **Owner** | the quarterly review: is the plan working | four times a year | 2 h/quarter |

**FACT-ish — the constraint that makes this necessary:** GH₵1,500 buys **about 30
hours** of work (`PRICING.md` §2). If support and admin eat into the build block,
the build does not finish, and the job that was priced at GH₵50/hour becomes
GH₵35/hour. **The calendar is the margin.**

**DECIDED — the non-negotiable:** the build block is uninterrupted. Messages are
answered twice a day, in a block, not continuously. Say so on the page so it is a
policy and not a slight.

### 3.1 Who is accountable for what

| Decision | Whose | Never delegated |
|---|---|---|
| the price | Caleb | — |
| whether to take the job | Caleb | — |
| what is in scope | Caleb, with the client's signature | — |
| what the client's domain points at | the client | never |
| what the tax return says | Caleb, checked by an accountant | — |
| whether a claim is true | the verifier | — |

**INFERENCE — the accountant row is the only one that should ever be bought in.**
Not because the work is hard, but because the penalty for being wrong is
disproportionate, and an accountant costs less than one GH₵500 late-filing fine.

## 6. The protocol: how a job actually runs

A **protocol** is the sequence. A **procedure** is the steps inside one stage. This
is the protocol, with the gate that stops each stage going wrong.

| # | Stage | Owner | Output | **Gate — do not pass until** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Enquiry** | Seller | a reply within 48 h on weekdays | the buyer can state what it is for |
| 2 | **Call** | Seller | 45 minutes, 6 questions | you know what "done" means to them |
| 3 | **Scope** | Seller | one page: pages, exclusions, price, date | **they sign it** |
| 4 | **Deposit** | Accountant | 50% received | **money is in the account, not promised** |
| 5 | **Content** | Builder | their text and images in hand | you have everything, or the date has moved |
| 6 | **Build** | Builder | the site, on a preview URL | it loads under 1 MB, ten widths, no overflow |
| 7 | **Review** | Support | one round of comments | the round is consolidated, not drip-fed |
| 8 | **Balance** | Accountant | 40% before the demo, 10% on handover | paid before the site is live |
| 9 | **Launch** | Builder | live, in their name | 2FA on, transfer lock on, headers verified |
| 10 | **Handover** | Operator | 30-min session + written sheet | they can change a price themselves |
| 11 | **Warranty** | Support | 30 days, bugs only | bug or change was named at the time |
| 12 | **Onboarding** | Support | months 1–3, one hour a month | the monthly note went out three times |
| 13 | **Plan** | Owner | month 4, GH₵200–400/mo | **they said yes, in writing** |

**The three gates that matter most, and why:**

1. **Gate 4 — money in the account.** A promised deposit is not a deposit. Every
   freelancer who has been burned was burned by starting on a promise.
2. **Gate 5 — content in hand.** Late content is the single most common reason a
   fixed-date job slips, and then the date is blamed on you. The scope says the
   date moves; say it out loud at gate 5.
3. **Gate 13 — an explicit yes.** Nothing recurring begins silently. This is the
   whole brand in one rule.

### 6.1 The six questions asked at every call

Every one of them exists because skipping it costs a rebuild.

1. What must a visitor be able to do in the first five seconds?
2. Who is the visitor, and what do they already know?
3. What is the one action — call, WhatsApp, visit, or buy?
4. Who writes the words, and who approves them?
5. What is the date, and what has to be true for it to be met?
6. **What must this never say?** *(the question nobody asks, and the one that
   produces the most useful answer)*

## 7. The rhythm: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annually

Efficiency in a one-person business is not speed. It is **not having to remember
anything**. Every recurring decision is put on a schedule so it stops consuming
attention.

| Cadence | Time | What | Why it is on this list |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | 45 min | messages, replies, quotes | the seller hat, before the build block |
| **Daily** | 4 h | the build block, phone off | the margin |
| **Daily** | 45 min | support and changes | the support hat |
| **Daily** | 5 min | log every payment and every expense | 5 minutes now, or three days in April |
| **Weekly** | 2 h | Friday operator block: books, files, backups, invoices | the week is closed before the weekend |
| **Monthly** | 2 h | close the month: reconcile, set aside tax, send the check notes | see §8 |
| **Quarterly** | 2 h | owner review: pipeline, plan conversion, one thing to stop | the only strategic thinking that happens |
| **Quarterly** | 30 min | security sweep: links, forms, SSL, DNS records | `SECURITY-HOSTING.md` |

**INFERENCE — the weekly block is the one that will be skipped and must not be.**
It is the only thing standing between a tidy business and a shoebox of receipts in
April. Two hours on Friday is cheaper than two days in April, and it is the same
trap every freelancer falls into.

## 8. Money: the part that decides whether this works

### 8.1 The chart of accounts — seven lines is enough

A sole trader does not need an accounting system. It needs seven categories that
map to a tax return and a bank statement.

| # | Account | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sales — builds** | GH₵1,500 and the larger offers |
| 2 | **Sales — plans** | GH₵200–400 monthly |
| 3 | **Direct costs** | domains bought on the client's behalf, if any, and nothing else |
| 4 | **Tools** | hosting (GH₵0), the phone's data share, any paid software |
| 5 | **Marketing** | printing, a paid post, anything to get a client |
| 6 | **Owner's draw** | what you actually pay yourself — **not an expense** |
| 7 | **Tax set-aside** | a separate account, funded monthly, touched only for GRA |

**INFERENCE — line 6 and line 7 are the two that freelancers get wrong.** A draw
is not a cost; treating it as one hides whether the business is profitable. And tax
that lives in the same account as rent is tax that gets spent.

### 8.2 The money rules

| Rule | Number | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Deposit** | 50% | before any work |
| **Balance** | 40% at demo, 10% at handover | `index.html` already says this |
| **Tax set-aside** | **3% of every payment, moved the day it lands** | matches the presumptive rate; if the actual bill is lower you have a buffer |
| **Buffer** | 3 months of personal costs | below this, you cannot say no to a bad job |
| **Maximum unpaid work** | one hour, once, per enquiry | the free call is a call, not a build |
| **Late payment** | stop work at day 14, in writing | said once, in the scope, so it is not a surprise |

**INFERENCE — the set-aside is 3% because that is the presumptive rate.** If the
actual route turns out to be the modified cash basis, 3% of *turnover* is more than
3% of *profit* — so the buffer works in your favour. See §8.3.

### 8.3 Tax: what is actually owed — and the trap

**FACT — the law changed on 1 September 2026.** The Income Tax (Amendment) Act,
2026 (**Act 1178**), assented 26 August 2026, took effect **1 September 2026**.

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Tax-free threshold** | **GH₵7,056 a year (GH₵588 a month)** |
| Presumptive — instalment (PTI) | average turnover ≤ **GH₵20,000** over three years |
| Presumptive — turnover (**PTT**) | turnover **GH₵20,000–750,000** → **3% of turnover** |
| Modified cash basis (MCB) | tax on **profit**, after allowable expenses |
| **Filing deadline** | **30 April** each year |
| **Late filing** | **GH₵500** + **GH₵10 per day** |
| Late payment | 125% of the statutory rate, compounded monthly |

**FACT — the withholding tax that nobody warns you about.** When a business client
pays for services, they deduct **7.5%** and give you a certificate:

| Payment type | Withholding |
|---|---|
| Goods | 3% |
| **Services** | **7.5%** |
| Works | 5% |

**INFERENCE — this is the single most practically important tax fact for
SoftFire.** A GH₵1,500 job invoiced to a business, a school or a church may arrive
as **GH₵1,387.50**, with a certificate for GH₵112.50. That is:

- **not a loss** — the certificates are a **tax credit** at year end;
- **but it is a cash-flow gap** if you expected the full number;
- and **if you lose the certificates, you pay the tax twice.**

**The rule that follows — DECIDED:** keep every withholding certificate in the job
folder, and never quote a price assuming the gross arrives.

**THE TRAP — FACT, and it may apply to you.** An individual with a **professional
qualification** is **excluded** from the presumptive routes (PTI and PTT) and must
use the modified cash basis or standard rules. Owners of multiple businesses and
partners in registered partnerships are excluded too.

**INFERENCE — so this is a question only Caleb can answer:**

> **Do you hold a formal qualification — a degree, a diploma, or membership of a
> professional body in IT or computing?** If yes, the 3% route may not be available
> to you, and the accounting is **profit-based**, which means **every receipt
> matters**.

That single question changes the record-keeping from "a sales book" to "a proper
set of books". It is the highest-value question in this document, and I will not
answer it for you.

**PROPOSED, pending that answer:** visit the nearest GRA office, describe the
business, and ask to be classified. Free, and it settles it.

### 8.4 Accounting: what "doing the books" means here

| Task | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Log the payment | the day it lands | date, client, invoice number, amount, MoMo or bank |
| Log the expense | the day it is paid | date, what, who, receipt photo |
| Match to the bank or MoMo statement | weekly | every line accounted for, or explained |
| Reconcile | monthly | statement closing balance = your closing balance |
| Set aside tax | monthly | 3% of the month's receipts, moved out |
| Check profit | monthly | receipts − costs, not receipts alone |
| Year-end pack | March | the year's lines, certificates, receipts, in one folder |

**INFERENCE — the accounting that matters most is not the tax return.** It is
knowing, in month three, that the GH₵1,500 job actually earned GH₵1,120 after 30
hours, the merchant fee and travel. A business that only learns that at year-end
has already made the decision wrong eight times.

### 8.5 Efficiency: where the 30 hours actually go

| Stage | Hours | Can it shrink? |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery and scope | 2 | no — this is the product |
| Structure | 2 | yes — a standard page plan per business type |
| Content placement | 3 | **yes — a worksheet the client fills in** |
| Build | 12 | **yes — this is the reusable system** |
| Test | 3 | yes — automate what the verifier already does |
| Domain and hosting | 2 | yes — a checklist, done once, then repeated |
| Handover | 2 | yes — a recorded video, reused |
| **Total** | **30** | **realistically 22–24 on the third job** |

**INFERENCE — the business only becomes viable at the third identical job.** The
first build of a business type costs 30 hours; the third costs 22 because the
structure, worksheet and checklist already exist. **That is why the goal is one
documented project, not ten quick ones** — the point of the first is to produce the
reusable parts.

**The three assets that make the next job cheaper:**

1. A **page-plan template** per business type (church, school, shop, service).
2. A **content worksheet** — the questions, so the client supplies words, not you.
3. A **launch checklist** — the 20 items from `SECURITY-HOSTING.md`, as a list.

None of these exist yet. **Building them after job one is the highest-return hour
in the business.**

## 9. Compliance calendar: the dates that cost money to miss

| Date | What | Cost of missing it |
|---|---|---|
| **30 April, annually** | personal income tax return | GH₵500 + GH₵10/day |
| **Annually** | business name renewal (ORC) | GH₵70, and the name can lapse |
| **Annually** | Business Operating Permit (MMDA) | varies; enforcement is local |
| **Annually, per client** | domain renewals | the site dies |
| **Monthly** | set aside tax, reconcile | a bad April |
| **Quarterly** | security sweep per client | a broken form nobody notices |
| **As it happens** | keep every withholding certificate | pay tax twice |

**FACT — formalising costs more than the ORC's GH₵130.** A 2026 ILAPI study of 600
enterprises found the average MMDA permit is **GH₵1,275** and licence fees average
**GH₵1,600** (up to GH₵3,000); registration averages **GH₵1,030** in practice. The
headline fee is not the cost.

**INFERENCE — sequence the formalisation, do not do it all at once:**

| Order | Step | Cost | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business name at ORC | **GH₵130 + GH₵60** | before the first invoice |
| 2 | TIN — free with the Ghana Card | GH₵0 | with step 1 |
| 3 | Confirm the GRA classification | GH₵0 | with step 1 |
| 4 | Separate MoMo or bank account for the business | GH₵0 | with step 1 |
| 5 | Business Operating Permit | **GH₵200–1,275** | **after the first paid job** |
| 6 | Consider a limited company | **GH₵585 + 1%** | only if a client requires it |

**INFERENCE — step 5 is the one to defer deliberately.** A permit is an annual
local cost that buys the right to trade from premises. Until there is a paying
client, it is a cost with no revenue behind it. **Deferring it is a decision; do
not drift into it.**

## 10. Risk: what can actually go wrong

Scored `likelihood × impact`, both 1–5. Anything 12 or above gets a rule, not a
hope.

| # | Risk | L | I | Score | The control |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No paying client for months** | 4 | 5 | **20** | 90-day goal is one job; price low enough to be reachable |
| 2 | **A job runs over 30 hours** | 4 | 4 | **16** | the scope's exclusion list; stop and re-quote at hour 30 |
| 3 | **Late payment** | 3 | 4 | **12** | 50% deposit, stop at day 14, in writing |
| 4 | **A client's account is compromised** | 2 | 5 | **10** | they own it, 2FA at launch, transfer lock |
| 5 | **Scope creep** | 5 | 2 | 10 | "bug or change" named before work |
| 6 | **Tax penalty** | 2 | 4 | 8 | 30 April on the calendar, 3% set aside |
| 7 | **Domain lost to a registrar lapse** | 2 | 4 | 8 | care plan watches expiry |
| 8 | **Illness — one person, no backup** | 3 | 4 | **12** | a written handover sheet per live site |
| 9 | **The product never ships** | 4 | 3 | **12** | it is the 3-year goal, not the 90-day one |
| 10 | **A price change breaks the ladder** | 3 | 2 | 6 | the verifier pins every row by name |
| 11 | **Reputation damage from one bad job** | 2 | 5 | 10 | refund or fix free; the warranty is 30 days |

### 10.1 The three risks that are actually existential

1. **No client (#1).** Everything else is survivable. This one is not. It is why
   GH₵1,500 is defensible even though it barely pays — a first paid job is worth
   more than a well-priced second one.
2. **Overrun (#2).** A GH₵1,500 job that takes 60 hours is a **GH₵25/hour** job and
   it destroys the month. The control is not discipline, it is the **exclusion
   list**, agreed before the deposit.
3. **Illness (#8).** One person, no backup. **The control is unglamorous and
   real:** every live site has a handover sheet in the folder — host, registrar,
   what to do if it breaks — so someone competent could step in for a week.

### 10.2 SWOT, without the flattery

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | published fixed prices nobody else publishes · a verifier that proves claims · ownership in the client's name · a documented process, not a pitch · low overhead — GH₵0 hosting |
| **Weaknesses** | **no paying client yet** · one person, no capacity and no backup · GH₵1,500 is thin · no case study · no registered company, which some buyers require |
| **Opportunities** | 92% of Ghanaian businesses are MSMEs and ~70% of GDP (**FACT**, ILAPI 2026) · the sector is crowded at the bottom and empty at documented process · diaspora and NGO buyers pay more · the retainer model is untouched locally |
| **Threats** | a competitor copies the price · a template builder gets good enough · the buyer does not read, and compares on price alone · GRA or MMDA costs land before revenue |

**INFERENCE — the honest read.** The strengths are real but they are all *process*
strengths, and process does not sell to someone who has never bought. The weakness
that matters is not skill, price or design — it is **the absence of one person who
has paid and would say so.** Every strategic decision for the next 90 days should
be judged by whether it produces that person.

## 11. Professionalism, defined concretely

"Professional" is used to mean well-dressed. Here it means six observable things.

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| replying within the time you promised, including "no" | replying fast to good news only |
| a scope the client can hold you to | a scope only you understand |
| telling them the cheaper option when it is cheaper | maximising each invoice |
| naming a mistake before they find it | defending a mistake |
| a written record of every decision | a memory of the call |
| finishing the boring part — the handover sheet, the certificate | shipping and moving on |

**INFERENCE — the cheapest professionalism is the two documents nobody sends:**
the handover sheet and the monthly note. They cost 20 minutes, they are the thing
the client keeps, and no competitor in the price table sends either.
