## 2. Governance — the constitution

Six clauses. They fit on a card. They are the whole of SoftFire's internal law.

1. **No price without a written scope, and no scope without exclusions.**
2. **No work before the deposit clears.**
3. **The client owns their accounts, their domain and their content. Always.**
4. **Nothing recurring starts without an explicit yes, in writing.**
5. **No claim that has not been measured or sourced — including in my own records.**
6. **If a rule above costs me the job, the rule wins.**

**Clause 6 is the only one that needs courage.** The other five are procedures. The
sixth is the one tested on a Thursday afternoon when a church with a real budget

## 3. Law — the six statutes that touch this business

| Law | What it requires of SoftFire | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843)** | register as a controller **before processing**; written processor contracts (s.30(2)); notify breaches (s.31); honour access and correction | **UNMET — do this first** |
| **Copyright Act, 2005 (Act 690)** | s.7: commissioned work vests in **the client** absent a contract; assignment must be **in writing and signed** (s.9(4)); moral rights stay with the author | write the clause |
| **Income Tax Act, 2015 (Act 896)**, as amended by **Act 1178 (2026)** | personal income tax; file by **30 April** | see §7 |
| **Revenue Administration Act, 2016 (Act 915)** | records; electronic filing; penalties | §7 |
| **Trade Marks Act, 2004 (Act 664)** | register the name if it is to be defended | `brand/BRAND.md` |
| **Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992)** | business name; annual renewal | GH₵130 + GH₵70/yr |

### 3.1 The two findings that change the business

**1. You are almost certainly an unregistered data controller, and 2026 is an
enforcement year.** Act 843 s.27(1) requires registration **before** processing;
s.53 says an unregistered controller must not process at all; s.56 makes it criminal
— up to 250 penalty units or two years, or both. The DPC's Executive Director has
said there are **"no exemptions"**; spot checks began **5 October 2026**.

A WhatsApp number is personal data. So is every contact form on every site you build
— which also makes **the client** the controller and **you** the processor, and
s.30(2) requires a **written agreement** for that.

**2. In Ghana the client owns the copyright by default — and almost no studio says
so.** Act 690 s.7 vests commissioned work in the **commissioner**. That is the
opposite of the US/UK default, and it makes the ownership promise the strongest
legally checkable claim on the page. State it, with the carve-out: SoftFire keeps the
reusable system, the client gets a permanent licence to what is on their site.

**A gift with a hidden condition is not a gift.**

## 5. POLICIES — the register

A **policy** is a standing decision with a reason and an owner. It is not a
procedure (which is steps) and not a rule (which is one line). Fifteen of them,
each with what breaks if it is ignored.

| # | Policy | It says | Why | If broken |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **Pricing** | published prices; **no discount, no launch price, no church price** | the price is the position | every sale becomes a negotiation |
| **P2** | **Scope** | one page signed, exclusions listed before work | fixed price needs a fixed boundary | the job runs 60 hours and earns GH₵25/h |
| **P3** | **Payment** | 50% before work, 40% at demo, 10% at handover; work stops at day 14 unpaid | a promise is not a payment | you fund the client's business |
| **P4** | **Refund** | deposit is not refundable once work starts; unused stages are | time was reserved | either side can walk at will |
| **P5** | **Warranty** | 30 days, **bugs only**; a bug is defined against the signed scope | where disputes start is the bug/change line | free redesign, forever |
| **P6** | **Ownership** | client owns accounts, content and the work; SoftFire keeps the reusable system and grants a permanent licence | it is the law *and* the promise | the next site cannot be built |
| **P7** | **Data protection** | register with the DPC; collect only what is needed; written processor agreement; incident log | Act 843, criminal exposure | a fine and a prosecution |
| **P8** | **Security** | 2FA and transfer lock on every client account; headers at launch | accounts, not the site, are the risk | a hijacked domain you cannot recover |
| **P9** | **Communication** | one channel (WhatsApp); replies within **48h on weekdays**, twice a day, not continuously | a promise you keep beats a faster one you miss | the build block dies |
| **P10** | **Records** | every job is a folder; every payment logged the day it lands; **append-only** | you cannot prove what you did not write | no defence, no margin, no tax claim |
| **P11** | **Retention** | job folders and certificates kept **7 years**; client data deleted on request | GRA and Act 843 both point this way | an audit with no records |
| **P12** | **Accessibility** | WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, 16px body, 44px targets, measured not assumed | the buyer's customer is on a cheap phone | a site some customers cannot use |
| **P13** | **Complaints** | named, written, answered within 48h; fix first, argue second | the cheapest reputation repair there is | a quiet client telling ten people |
| **P14** | **Conflicts** | quote the smallest offer that works; name a free fix as a fix; never discount | the temptation is always available | the position quietly dissolves |
| **P15** | **Subcontractors** | none today. If one is used: named in the scope, bound by P7 and P16 | **written now so it is not improvised** | a subcontractor's mistake becomes your liability |

| **P16** | **Change control** | no policy changes in a live client's favour without being written down with a date | a rule that bends silently is not a rule | the standard dissolves one exception at a time |

**INFERENCE — P15 is deliberately a policy about nothing.** The value of writing
"no subcontractors today" is that the day a job overruns and a friend offers to help,
the decision has already been made in a calm week.

## 7. Accounting and tax

### 7.1 Seven accounts is enough

Sales — builds · Sales — plans · Direct costs · Tools · Marketing · **Owner's draw** ·
**Tax set-aside**

**Two lines freelancers get wrong:** a draw is **not** an expense (treating it as
one hides whether the business is profitable), and tax in the same account as rent
gets spent.

### 7.2 The tax rules

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tax-free threshold** | **GH₵7,056/yr** (Act 1178, effective 1 Sep 2026) |
| Presumptive — instalment | average turnover ≤ GH₵20,000 over three years |
| Presumptive — turnover | GH₵20,000–**750,000** → **3% of turnover** |
| Modified cash basis | tax on **profit** after allowable expenses |
| **Filing** | **30 April** |
| **Late filing** | **GH₵500 + GH₵10/day** |
| **Withholding on services** | **7.5%** — the client deducts it and gives you a certificate |

**The withholding trap:** a GH₵1,500 invoice to a business can arrive as
**GH₵1,387.50**. Not a loss — certificates are a tax credit — but a cash-flow gap.
**Lose the certificates and you pay the tax twice.**

**THE OPEN QUESTION —** an individual with a **professional qualification** is
**excluded** from the presumptive routes and must use the modified cash basis. **Do
you hold a formal IT or computing qualification?** If yes, the books must be
profit-based and every receipt matters. *I will not answer this for you. Ask GRA;
it is free.*

### 7.3 Efficiency — where the 30 hours go

| Stage | Hours | Shrinkable? |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery and scope | 2 | no — this is the product |
| Structure | 2 | yes — a standard page plan |
| Content placement | 3 | **yes — a worksheet the client fills** |
| Build | 12 | **yes — the reusable system** |
| Test | 3 | yes — automate via the verifier |
| Domain and hosting | 2 | yes — a checklist |
| Handover | 2 | yes — a recorded video |
| **Total** | **30** | **22–24 by the third job** |

**The business only becomes viable at the third identical job.** The three assets

yet. **Building them after job one is the highest-return hour in the business.**

## 9. Operations — how a job runs

### 9.1 The protocol, with the gates

| # | Stage | Output | **Gate — do not pass until** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enquiry | reply within 48h | the buyer can state what it is for |
| 2 | Call | 45 min, six questions | you know what "done" means to them |
| 3 | Scope | one page: sections, exclusions, price, date | **they sign it** |
| 4 | Deposit | 50% received | **money is in the account, not promised** |
| 5 | Content | their text and images in hand | you have everything, or the date has moved |
| 6 | Build | a preview URL | under 1 MB, ten widths, no overflow |
| 7 | Review | one consolidated round | the round is consolidated, not drip-fed |
| 8 | Balance | 40% at demo, 10% at handover | paid before the site is live |
| 9 | Launch | live, in their name | 2FA on, transfer lock on, headers verified |
| 10 | Handover | 30-min session + written sheet | they can change a price themselves |
| 11 | Warranty | 30 days, bugs only | bug or change was named at the time |
| 12 | Onboarding | months 1–3, 1 hour/month | the monthly note went out three times |
| 13 | Plan | GH₵200–400/mo | **they said yes, in writing** |

**The three gates that matter:** money *in the account*; content *in hand*; an
explicit **yes** before anything recurring.

### 9.2 The six questions at every call

1. What must a visitor be able to do in the first five seconds?
2. Who is the visitor, and what do they already know?
3. What is the one action — call, WhatsApp, visit, buy?
4. Who writes the words, and who approves them?
5. What is the date, and what must be true for it to be met?
6. **What must this never say?**

### 9.3 The ten-question screen, before accepting any job

1. Can they state what "done" means in one sentence?
2. Is the buyer the person who signs?
3. Is the budget at or above the published price?
4. Is the content available, or is someone writing it?
5. Is there one decision-maker, or a committee?
6. Does this need a database, a login or payments? *(if yes, it is a different offer)*
7. Can I meet the date within 30 hours?
8. Will the deposit clear before I start?
9. Are they willing to own the accounts?
10. **Would I be comfortable if this client published the entire email thread?**

## 12. Maintenance, hosting, security

| | |
|---|---|
| **Warranty** | days 1–30, bugs only, free |
| **Onboarding** | months 1–3, 1 hour/month + a monthly note, included |
| **Care plan** | month 4 on, GH₵200–400/mo, only on an explicit yes |
| **If nothing ever changes** | pay nothing — a static site needs no maintenance |

**Maintenance asks "is it still working?" Management asks "is it still true?"** A
static site has no database, no plugins, no CMS: **the real work is two hours a
year.** Selling a maintenance retainer on it would charge for work that does not
exist.

**Hosting is GH₵0, named, in the client's account.** Domain GH₵146–260/yr paid
directly to the registrar. **No resale** — it gives up ~GH₵1,200/yr per client and
buys the one claim no competitor can make.

**Security is a launch checklist, not a subscription:** 2FA, registrar lock, headers,
SPF/DKIM/DMARC. Nine of twenty standard controls apply; seven happen once.
**GitHub Pages cannot send headers** — `_headers` works on Cloudflare Pages, which is
the recommended move.

**The trap:** the moment a site stops being static — a portal, a login, a database —
all of this inverts. **The retainer must follow the attack surface, not the budget.**

### 13.1 Five stages, and what each says to stop

| Stage | Bottleneck | Stop doing |

### 13.2 Strategy is a list of refusals

| Ranking, lead and growth promises | the easiest sale in the market |
| **Any discount** | the awkward first conversation |

**The test:** a competitor could not copy it without losing money. "We care more"

### 13.3 Scale's three levers

| Lever | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Price** | available now — GH₵3,000 at three case studies **doubles revenue on identical hours** |

### 13.4 Sustain — one number

**Three months of personal costs.** Not a luxury: it is the price of the only

accept every job does not have a strategy, it has a queue.

## 16. Professionalism — six observable things

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| replying within the time you promised, including "no" | replying fast to good news only |
| a scope the client can hold you to | a scope only you understand |
| telling them the cheaper option when it is cheaper | maximising each invoice |
| naming a mistake before they find it | defending a mistake |
| a written record of every decision | a memory of the call |
| finishing the boring part — the handover sheet, the certificate | shipping and moving on |

**The cheapest professionalism is the two documents nobody sends:** the handover
sheet and the monthly note. Twenty minutes each. No competitor in the price table
sends either.
