# Build, grow, scale, stabilise, sustain

The lifecycle of a one-person studio, the strategy behind it, and the calendar that
makes it true. Researched and computed **2026-11-06**.

Everything numeric here is **INFERENCE from numbers already in this repo** — the
30-hour build, the 4-hour block, the published prices. Marked accordingly.

---

## The number that decides everything

Before any advice about growth, this. It is arithmetic, and it is not negotiable by
wanting it more.

**Capacity, from `OPERATIONS.md`:** a build block of 4 hours a day, 5 days a week =
**20 build-hours per week.**

| | Builds per month | Gross from builds |
|---|---|---|
| First jobs (30 h each) | **2.9** | **GH₵4,330/mo** |
| Mature jobs (22 h each) | **3.9** | **GH₵5,905/mo** |

**SoftFire cannot build its way past about GH₵4,300 a month.** That is the ceiling,
and it is a good ceiling for one person — but it is a ceiling, and every growth
decision has to be made knowing it.

| Mix | Monthly | Yearly |
|---|---|---|
| 2 builds, no retainers | GH₵3,000 | GH₵36,000 |
| **2 builds + 10 retainers** | **GH₵5,000** | **GH₵60,000** |
| 3 builds + 10 retainers | GH₵6,500 | GH₵78,000 |

**INFERENCE — three conclusions follow, and they are the rest of this document:**

1. **The retainer is the only thing that raises the ceiling without more hours.**
   At 10 clients it adds GH₵2,000/mo for roughly 10 hours of support — work that
   already has a block reserved for it.
2. **Adding builds past three is impossible, not unwise.** There are only 20 hours.
3. **Therefore "scale" cannot mean "more projects".** It has to mean *more per
   hour* or *the same thing sold twice*.

## 1. The five stages, and what changes at each

Most business advice describes one stage and calls it the whole journey. Each stage
below has a different bottleneck and a different thing to stop doing.

### Stage 1 — BUILD (now): prove the thing works

| | |
|---|---|
| **The question** | can I deliver what I promised, at the price I promised? |
| **Bottleneck** | no paying client — the whole brand is currently a claim |
| **The goal** | **one paid project, documented** |
| **What to do** | the first three jobs at any reasonable price; record every hour actually spent |
| **What to stop** | designing anything new. Use the system that already exists |
| **Exit test** | one client paid, one case study exists, real hours are known |

**INFERENCE — the single most valuable output of stage 1 is not money.** It is the
**real hours number.** Every figure in this repo assumes 30; if the truth is 45,
the whole model changes and it is better to know that on job one than job ten.

### Stage 2 — STABILISE: make it repeatable

| | |
|---|---|
| **The question** | does it work the same way the second time, without me re-deciding? |
| **Bottleneck** | everything is bespoke; each job starts from zero |
| **The goal** | the three reusable assets exist |
| **What to do** | write the **page plan**, the **content worksheet**, the **launch checklist** |
| **What to stop** | improvising the process on each job |
| **Exit test** | a second job of the same type takes **22 hours, not 30** |

**INFERENCE — this is the stage everybody skips, and it is the only one that
compounds.** A studio that goes from job 1 to job 10 without stabilising does job 10
in 30 hours too, and has learned nothing it can charge for.

### Stage 3 — GROW: get to ten retainers

| | |
|---|---|
| **The question** | can I turn builds into relationships? |
| **Bottleneck** | acquisition — nothing currently produces a lead on purpose |
| **The goal** | **10 clients on the Care Plan** |
| **What to do** | publish the work · ask every client at month 3 · one channel, done properly |
| **What to stop** | waiting for referrals to happen by accident |
| **Exit test** | GH₵2,000/mo recurring, and it arrives without a new sales conversation |

**INFERENCE — 10 is not an arbitrary target.** Support capacity is **16 hours a
month** at one hour per client; 10 leaves room for a bad month. At 16 clients the
retainer starts eating the build block, and the thing that acquires clients stops.

### Stage 4 — SCALE: raise the price or sell the same thing twice

| | |
|---|---|
| **The question** | how do I earn more without more hours? |
| **Bottleneck** | the ceiling in §"the number" — GH₵4,300 from builds, forever |
| **The goal** | the **product** (`BRAND.md` vision) or a **higher price** |
| **What to do** | see §3 — there are only three levers |
| **What to stop** | believing more effort is a growth strategy |
| **Exit test** | revenue rises in a month where hours did not |

### Stage 5 — SUSTAIN: make it survive you

| | |
|---|---|
| **The question** | what happens in a bad month, or a bad year? |
| **Bottleneck** | one person, no buffer, no backup |
| **The goal** | three months of costs held; a handover sheet per client |
| **What to do** | see §5 |
| **What to stop** | spending the retainer money as if it were build money |
| **Exit test** | you can say no to a bad job |

**INFERENCE — the stages are not a ladder you climb once.** A bad month sends the
business back to stage 1. The point of stages 2 and 5 is that they make the return
trip shorter.

## 2. Strategy: what it actually is

**Strategy is not a plan. Strategy is a list of things you will not do.**

A plan says *what to do next*. Strategy says *what to refuse even when it is
offered*, and every real strategy costs money the day it is adopted.

**SoftFire's strategy, in five refusals:**

| Refused | What it costs | Why it is worth it |
|---|---|---|
| **More than 3 builds a month** | the fourth client's money | 20 hours is the hard limit; the fourth build is the first late one |
| **Bespoke design per client** | the joy of it, and the portfolio variety | only reuse gets a build to 22 hours |
| **Reselling hosting** | ~GH₵1,200/yr per client | it is the one claim no competitor can make |
| **Ranking, lead and growth promises** | the easiest sale in the market | none of them can be measured honestly |
| **Clients below the published price** | the awkward first conversation | a price that moves once has moved forever |

**INFERENCE — the test of a strategy is that a competitor could not copy it without
losing money.** Every row above passes that test, because every row is something a
competitor would have to give up revenue to match. *"We care more"* fails the test.
*"We will not resell your hosting"* passes it.

### 2.1 Tactics: the specific moves, per stage

Strategy is stable. Tactics change by stage. These are the moves, not the principles.

| Stage | Tactic | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| Build | **offer the first three clients a stated discount** — GH₵1,000 instead of GH₵1,500, in writing, with an end date | honest, dated, and buys the case study |
| Build | **time every hour of job one** | it is the number the whole model rests on |
| Stabilise | **write the assets on job one, not after job three** | the memory of what went wrong is only fresh once |
| Grow | **ask at month 3, not month 1** | by then the client has used the plan and knows what it is |
| Grow | **one channel: WhatsApp + the published work** | there is no capacity to run two |
| Scale | **raise the price before hiring** | see §3 |
| Sustain | **hold three months of costs** | the only thing that lets you say no |

**INFERENCE — the tactic that matters most right now is the stated discount.** It
is the only move available that turns the brand from a claim into evidence, and it
is honest because it is written down with an end date. A silent discount would
destroy the position; a published one is a launch offer.

## 3. Scale: the three levers, and the one that is wrong

There are exactly three ways to earn more without more hours. Only two are
available to this business today.

### Lever 1 — RAISE THE PRICE (available now)

**INFERENCE — the cheapest growth in the business, and the least used.** At the
capacity ceiling, price is the only variable left:

| Price | 2.9 builds/mo | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| GH₵1,500 | GH₵4,330 | a portfolio nobody has yet |
| **GH₵3,000** | **GH₵8,660** | **three documented case studies** |
| GH₵5,000 | GH₵14,430 | a waiting list, or a product |

**The rule — PROPOSED:** raise the price after **three** documented jobs, not
before, and never mid-client. The published price changes between clients, in
public, on the page, with the date. That is consistent with everything else here:
*a number that changes is fine, as long as it is written down when it changes.*

### Lever 2 — SELL THE SAME THING TWICE (the stated vision)

The product. It is why the 3-year goal is what it is. **INFERENCE:** a school
portal sold to five schools at GH₵14,999 is **GH₵75,000** of work that is built
once — and it is the only path past the ceiling that does not require hiring.

**The trap, stated plainly:** the product is *not* a fifth offer to sell. It is a
separate business with its own support burden, its own law (Act 843 gets much
harder with student data), and its own price. **A one-person studio cannot build
the product and run the service at full capacity at the same time.** Something has
to give, and the honest plan is that builds become the minority of the week.

### Lever 3 — HIRE (wrong, for now)

**INFERENCE — hiring at GH₵1,500 a build is arithmetic that does not work.**

| | |
|---|---|
| A build earns | GH₵1,500 |
| A junior's monthly cost | likely **more than one build** |
| Builds needed to cover one hire | **3–4 a month** |
| Build capacity with one hire | still capped by *their* hours, plus your management time |
| Your new job | sales and management, which you have never done |

**The maths:** to keep a hire busy and paid you need volume you cannot yet generate,
at a price that leaves nothing over. **The sequencing is wrong, not the ambition** —
hire *after* the price rises, not before.

**The correct order — INFERENCE, and this is the strategic sentence of the
document:**

> **Raise the price, then hire. Never hire to justify the price you have.**

### 3.1 What "scale" must never mean here

| Not this | Because |
|---|---|
| more clients at the same price | it is the same hours with more admin |
| an agency with a team | GH₵1,500 does not carry a payroll |
| taking every job | the calendar is the margin |
| growing traffic or followers | neither has produced a sale in this market |

## 4. Scheduling: the calendar is the strategy

A strategy that is not on a calendar is a wish. This is the actual week.

### 4.1 The week

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning** (45 min) | messages, quotes | messages | messages | messages | messages |
| **Build block** (4 h) | one job | one job | one job | one job | **no build** |
| **Support** (45 min) | changes | changes | changes | changes | changes |
| **Friday block** (2 h) | — | — | — | — | **books, files, invoices, notes** |

**Why Friday has no build block — INFERENCE:** if building is available on Friday,
it fills Friday, and the week is never closed. **The gap is the control.** A calendar
with no slack is a calendar that fails on the first sick day.

### 4.2 The job schedule, from signature to paid

| Day | What | Who must act |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 0** | scope signed, deposit requested | client |
| **Day 1** | deposit clears — **work starts only now** | client |
| **Days 1–3** | content worksheet sent and filled | **client — this is the usual delay** |
| **Days 4–12** | the build, in the block | SoftFire |
| **Day 13** | demo, one consolidated comment round | client |
| **Day 15** | changes applied, 40% invoiced | SoftFire |
| **Day 17** | handover session, 10% invoiced, live | both |
| **Day 18–48** | the 30-day warranty | SoftFire |
| **Day 90** | the plan conversation | SoftFire |

**INFERENCE — Days 1–3 are where the schedule actually dies.** Every late launch in
a small studio traces to content arriving after the build started. The scope says
the date moves; the calendar says it in days.

### 4.3 The capacity calendar — the one that prevents overrun

Reserve two build slots a month, not three.

| | Slots |
|---|---|
| Theoretical capacity | 2.9–3.9 builds/month |
| **Booked capacity** | **2** |
| The third | for overrun, illness, and the client who appears |

**INFERENCE — booking 2 of 3 is not laziness, it is the only way the date holds.**
A studio booked to capacity misses the first date it is tested on, and the whole
position is built on dates holding. **The gap between 2 and 3 is what "the number
does not move" is made of.**

## 5. Sustain: what makes it last

Sustain is not growth's opposite. It is the thing that makes growth reversible.

| # | The sustain item | The number | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cash buffer** | **3 months of personal costs** | below this you cannot refuse a bad job |
| 2 | **Client concentration** | no client above **30%** of revenue | one client leaving should not be a crisis |
| 3 | **Recurring share** | **30–40%** of revenue | the mix that survives a quiet month |
| 4 | **Hours per build** | known and tracked | the number the price is built on |
| 5 | **One live project at a time** | never two overlapping builds | 20 hours does not stretch |
| 6 | **A handover sheet per client** | 100% | illness is a real risk, scored 12/25 |

**INFERENCE — item 1 is the whole of sustain.** Three months of costs is not a
luxury; it is the price of the only strategic freedom that matters, which is
**the ability to say no.** A business that must accept every job does not have a
strategy, it has a queue.

### 5.1 The 5-year arithmetic

Projected, not promised — **INFERENCE**, assuming the plan holds.

| Year | Builds/mo | Price | Retainers | Monthly | Yearly |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | GH₵1,500 | 3 | GH₵3,600 | **GH₵43,200** |
| 2 | 2 | GH₵2,500 | 8 | GH₵6,600 | **GH₵79,200** |
| 3 | 2 | GH₵3,000 | 10 | GH₵8,000 | **GH₵96,000** |
| 4–5 | fewer builds | GH₵3,000 | 10 + product | GH₵8,000 + product | **the product decides** |

**INFERENCE — read the right-hand column carefully.** The build revenue barely
moves across five years, because capacity does not move. **Every cedi of growth
after year 1 comes from price and retainers, and after year 3, from the product.**
That is not a limitation of ambition; it is what one person with a 4-hour block can
honestly do.

## 6. The entrepreneurship part: what nobody tells you

Six things that are true and are not in the frameworks.

1. **The first client is worth more than the tenth.** Not the money — the evidence.
   Every claim on the page is currently a promise; one paid job converts all of them
   at once. Price the first three accordingly.
2. **Your capacity is your product.** A studio that can only do 3 a month and says
   so is more trustworthy than one claiming unlimited. Scarcity stated honestly
   reads as confidence.
3. **The boring part is the moat.** The verifier, the handover sheet, the monthly
   note, the incident log — nobody copies these because they are not fun, and they
   are exactly what a client remembers at renewal.
4. **Say no early and cheaply.** The cheapest refusal is at the scope stage, two
   hours in. The most expensive is after the deposit, when it is a refund and a
   relationship.
5. **A business that needs you every hour is a job.** The retainer and the product
   are the two things that could make it a business. Neither exists yet.
6. **Do not confuse motion with progress.** Three cheap jobs feel like a business
   and produce no assets. One documented job feels slower and produces the three
   assets the next ten depend on.
