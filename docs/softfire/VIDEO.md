# The video — three cuts, one spine

**Written 2026-11-06.** There is no video yet. This is the script, the shot list, and
what each shot requires. You said you can film it or generate it with AI; every shot
is tagged so you can see which is which.

---

## 0. The decision that comes before the script

**INFERENCE — a 15-second clip and a 5-minute video are not the same video cut shorter.**
They are two different products for two different people:

| | 15 s | 60 s | 5 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who watches** | someone scrolling WhatsApp Status | someone who has your link | someone who was *sent* it — a church committee, a school board, a relative |
| **Where** | phone, vertical, sound off | phone, vertical, mostly sound off | laptop or phone, sound on, sitting down |
| **Job** | make them ask "how much?" | make them message | make them agree in a meeting |
| **Watch-through, honest** | ~60–80% | ~25–40% | ~2% of cold visitors |
| **Cost of getting it wrong** | nothing | little | it is the one a committee watches together |

**The 5-minute video is not a marketing asset.** It is a substitute for you being in
the room. Its audience is already interested, so it can afford to be slow, and it
should be the *least* persuasive of the three.

**FACT — the sound-off constraint drives everything.** Meta's own captioning research
and every platform's autoplay default agree: most mobile video is watched with the
sound off. So **the words on screen carry the entire message alone.** Voiceover is a
bonus, never the carrier. Burn the text in; do not rely on platform captions.

---

## 1. The spine — four facts, in this order, in all three cuts

Every cut is the same four sentences, differently padded:

1. **What it is.** A five-section website for a church, school, shop or small business.
2. **What it costs.** GH₵1,500. Fixed before work starts. It does not move.
3. **What you get.** Five sections, live in 2–4 days, the domain in your name, the
   first year of hosting, a 30-day warranty on anything that breaks.
4. **What to do.** Message on WhatsApp and say what the business does. That is enough.

**Rule inherited from the hero (MANUAL §11.2):** one ask, said once, said last. No
video ends with "follow us, like, share and subscribe."

**The label rule, and it is not optional.** BiteKasa is a family business, built
unpaid. That is stated **on screen, in the first ten seconds, every time the footage
appears.** Unlabelled family work is the fastest way to lose a buyer who asks one
follow-up question — a buyer will ask, and the answer has to already be on screen.

## 2. The 15-second cut — WhatsApp Status

**Format** 1080×1920, 9:16, 30 fps. **Sound** none. **Duration** 15.0 s exactly —
Status clips loop, and a clip that ends mid-word loops badly.

Five beats, three seconds each. Nothing else. At three seconds a viewer reads about
seven words, so every card is under seven words.

| Time | On screen | What you see | How to make it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0–3.0 | **YOUR BUSINESS HAS NO WEBSITE.** | black field, cream type, one ember hairline rising | **[AI]** or After Effects — a text card, nothing to render |
| 3.0–6.0 | **GH₵1,500.** | the number, gold, filling the frame | **[AI]** — motion only. Never let a generator invent a number. Type it yourself. |
| 6.0–9.0 | **Five sections. Live in 2–4 days.** | the BiteKasa site actually scrolling on a phone | **[SCREEN]** — screen-record your own phone. AI cannot film a site that must be real. |
| 9.0–12.0 | **In your name. Fixed. No deposit to see a quote.** | handover screen: the domain, the login, in the client's name | **[SCREEN]** — blur any real credentials first |
| 12.0–15.0 | **WhatsApp 057 057 2221** · *Say what the business does.* | the number, held still, nothing moving | **[AI]** text card |

**The small print, burned in for the whole 6–12 s window, 28 px, muted:**
`BiteKasa — family business, built unpaid. One project shown, not a portfolio.`

**Why the price is beat two and not beat five.** A viewer who does not see a number in
the first six seconds assumes there is not one, and "contact us for pricing" is the
thing this brand exists to not do. **INFERENCE:** in a Status feed, the price is the
only thing that survives a half-second glance.

**What not to do:** no logo open. No music with lyrics (Status is often played in a
room with other people). No before/after wipe — there is no "before" you own the
rights to show.

## 3. The 60-second cut

**Format** 1080×1920, 9:16 (shoot 16:9 and crop safe, so the same footage gives you a
landscape version for the website). **Sound** optional voiceover; the text must work
without it. **Duration** 60 s.

Nine beats, ~6.5 s each.

| # | Time | Voiceover (if used) | On-screen text | Shot | How |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–6 | "Most small businesses in Ghana do not have a website. Not because they cannot afford one." | **NO WEBSITE.** | a closed shopfront at dusk, shutter down | **[FILM]** phone, natural light |
| 2 | 6–13 | "Because nobody told them the number, or what happens after they ask." | **NOBODY SAID THE NUMBER.** | close on a hand unlocking a shutter | **[FILM]** |
| 3 | 13–20 | — | **GH₵1,500.** / *Fixed before work starts. It does not move.* | black, number only | **[AI]** text card |
| 4 | 20–27 | "Five sections. Written for the person who is deciding whether to buy from you." | **Five sections.** | the live site, scrolling, thumb visible | **[SCREEN]** |
| 5 | 27–34 | "Live in two to four days." | **2–4 days.** | the same scroll, faster, time-lapse feel | **[SCREEN]** |
| 6 | 34–41 | "The domain is bought in your name. Not mine. The first year of hosting is included." | **Your name. Your accounts. Day one.** | a laptop showing a registrar checkout with the client's name | **[SCREEN]** — use a real demo domain you own |
| 7 | 41–48 | "Twenty-nine days of fixes if anything breaks, then it is yours." — say **thirty**, not twenty-nine | **30-day warranty.** | the site on three screen sizes at once | **[SCREEN]** |
| 8 | 48–54 | "What is not included: copywriting from scratch, photography, and anything that takes a card payment online. It is on page one of the quote, not page nine." | **What it is not.** / copy · photos · card payments | a printed quote, page one, exclusions circled in pencil | **[FILM]** |
| 9 | 54–60 | "Message me and say what the business does. That is enough to begin." | **WhatsApp 057 057 2221** / *Say what the business does.* | number held still | **[AI]** |

**Burn in from 20 s onward, bottom third, small:**
`BiteKasa — family business, built unpaid. One project shown.`

**The beat that earns the most trust is 8.** Every competitor's video lists what you
get. Almost none lists what you do not get — and the exclusion is the only part a
sceptical buyer believes, because it costs you something to say.

## 4. The 5-minute cut

**Format** 1920×1080. **Sound** on, voiceover throughout. **Duration** 5:00. Not for
cold traffic — this is the file you send when a committee asks "can you explain this
properly?"

### Chapter list

| Time | Chapter | Content | Shot | How |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **What this is** | One sentence, then the price, in the first fifteen seconds. No logo, no music swell. | talking head or text card | **[FILM]** phone on a tripod at eye level |
| 0:20 | **Who it is for** | The four buyers: church, school, shop, small business. One line each on the problem — members and giving; fees and results; orders and stock; not being findable. | four text cards, no stock photos | **[AI]** type only |
| 1:00 | **What GH₵1,500 buys** | The five sections, named. The domain, the hosting, the warranty. Read the actual inclusions list. | screen recording of the offer block | **[SCREEN]** |
| 2:00 | **What it does not buy** | The six exclusions, read out in full, at normal speed, no apology. Then: *"If the list is not what you wanted, tell me before we start, not after."* | the printed quote, page one | **[FILM]** |
| 2:45 | **A real build, start to finish** | BiteKasa: what it was, the constraint (no checkout, no account, no card — one tap opens WhatsApp), the decision, the outcome. **Label it: family business, built unpaid.** | the live site, scrolled slowly | **[SCREEN]** |
| 4:00 | **Handover** | The keys: domain, hosting, source, every login. *"You own everything on day one. Not a licence, not a monthly rental."* | the handover slide from the site | **[SCREEN]** |
| 4:30 | **What happens when you message** | The 48-hour written quote. No deposit to see it. No call to book. Then: *"If I am not the right person for the job, I will say so."* | talking head, same setup as 0:00 | **[FILM]** |
| 4:50 | **The number** | WhatsApp, held for ten seconds. Nothing else on screen. | text card | **[AI]** |

**INFERENCE — chapter 4:00 is the one that closes.** Every other builder in this market
talks about what they will do. Almost none talks about what the client holds the day
it ends. Ownership is the differentiator that costs nothing to promise and everything
to fake, which is why it converts.

## 5. Can AI make this?

Straight answer, because it decides your budget:

| Shot type | AI can do it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Text and motion cards** — the price, the number, the four buyers | **Yes, well** | flat type on a colour field is exactly what generators are good at. Keep every number out of the prompt and add it in an editor; generators invent digits. |
| **The product on screen** | **No** | AI cannot show a website. Anything generated is a drawing of a website, and a buyer who asks "can I see it?" finds nothing behind it. Screen-record the real thing — it is free. |
| **A person speaking** | **No — do not** | A generated face talking about a one-person business is the single fastest way to destroy the one advantage you have: that a real, reachable person does the work. Film yourself on a phone. It will look worse and work better. |
| **B-roll — shutters, hands, a street, a phone on a table** | **Partly** | ambient shots are fine to generate, but real footage of Accra costs a walk and looks truer than anything a model produces. |

**The recommendation — PROPOSED:** film the human shots on a phone (four hours, one
afternoon), capture the product with a screen recorder, and generate only the eight
text cards. That is a complete 60-second video for the cost of an afternoon and the
price of a screen recorder, with nothing in it that can be caught out.

## 6. Production card

Everything here comes from the site's own tokens, so the video and the page look like
the same hand made them.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Field** | `#16100D` charcoal — never pure black |
| **Ink** | `#F2E9E4` cream |
| **Accent** | `#F4A300` gold — the number, and only the number |
| **Interaction** | `#E85D04` orange — rules, arrows, progress |
| **Deep** | `#8C1C13` cherry — surfaces only, never type |
| **Muted** | `#A8968D` — the small print |
| **Type** | Inter, semibold for statements, regular for the small print |
| **Numbers** | JetBrains Mono, tabular — the price must be seen as data, not prose |
| **Motion** | one move per card, 200 ms, ease-out. No bounce, no spin, no zoom-punch |
| **Safe area** | keep all type 12% clear of every edge — Status crops the top and bottom |
| **Captions** | burned in, always, never platform-generated |

**One rule that covers the rest — the flame, in motion.** The gradient on the page runs
ember → orange → gold, which is a fire going from base to heat. In video, that
direction means one thing: **things get brighter as they get closer to the ask.** The
number is gold because it is the hottest point. Never run the gradient backwards, and
never put gold on something that is not the offer.
