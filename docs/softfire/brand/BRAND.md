# SoftFire — the brand, in five parts

Every line is marked **FACT** (traceable to the repo or a named source),
**INFERENCE** (a conclusion drawn from facts, arguable), or **UNKNOWN** (nobody
has told me, and I will not invent it). Prices are in GH₵. No testimonial, client,
follower count, award or case result appears anywhere in this document.

---

## 1. Brand strategy and foundation

### Purpose — FACT (from the site, written by Caleb)

> "The plan before the pixels."

Not a tagline invented for this document: it is the h1 on the live page, and the
whole six-document section exists to make it true. The purpose, stated plainly:
**a small organisation should be able to buy software the way it buys anything
else — knowing the price, the date, and what happens if it goes wrong.**

### Mission — FACT

> "Websites, portals and brand systems for churches, schools, shops and small
> businesses. Built, deployed and handed over by one person — with the keys in
> your name." (index.html, hero lede)

### Vision — FACT (answered by Caleb, 2026-11-06)

> **Build the studio up, then ship one repeatable product and sell that — not an
> endless supply of bespoke projects.**

The product is not named. The candidate the evidence points at is a portal for
schools: it is the only offer on the ladder flagged *Lead*, and the only one a
single operator can sell repeatedly without rebuilding it (GH₵14,999, 6–8 weeks).

**Why this vision is worth having — INFERENCE:** at GH₵1,500 a site, a one-person
studio has a hard ceiling and a low one — revenue is bounded by hours, and every
project starts from zero. This vision is not optional at that price; it is the
only route out. A
product breaks that ceiling once. It also explains the ladder below: the cheap
offers are not loss-leaders, they are ways to learn what the product should be.

**What it forbids:** SoftFire cannot sell itself as an agency that scales with
headcount. It will never say "our team of fifteen". Growth comes from a product,
not from people — which is why *"one person, named"* survives as a value rather
than being a stage to grow out of.

### Core values — FACT (each is already enforced by the site or the repo)

| Value | The evidence that it is real, not aspirational |
|---|---|
| **Written before built** | A one-page signed scope ships before work starts; the Build Plan offer is GH₵599 credited against the build |
| **One person, named** | "Caleb Bentum" is on the page; there is no "team" claim anywhere |
| **You own everything** | "100% in your name, in writing" — domain, hosting, source, accounts |
| **Say when it is a no** | A visible *"What I will not do"* list: no banking-grade fintech, no certified health data, no date one person cannot deliver, no unconfirmed result |
| **Never invent a result** | verify-claims.py fails the build if a claim has no source file. 77 checks |
| **Tell you the cheaper answer** | "I will tell you when the simpler answer is the right one, even when the simpler answer earns me less" |

**INFERENCE:** these six are the brand. Not the palette, not the flame. The
palette is how it is dressed; these are what it will not trade.

### Target audience — FACT (stated) + INFERENCE (sharpened)

FACT: churches, schools, shops, small businesses; any size; "the number of people
don't matter"; delivered everywhere. Accra/Spintex was removed as an example, so
location is not a qualifier.

INFERENCE — the real qualifying line is not sector, it is **record-keeping
state**:

| They are | Because | What they can buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Running on memory and DMs** | The owner is the database | Website, or Start here |
| **Running on Excel and paper** | Two people reconcile by hand every month | Portal, Full System |
| **Already have a site nobody can update** | They paid once and were left | Care Plan, or a rebuild |

That is testable: one question — *"where does the list live today?"* — sorts
every enquiry into one of three columns. The site's four-audience section already
mirrors this.

### Unique value proposition — INFERENCE (defensible, and narrow)

**"A finished, working system with a written price, from the one person who built
it — in a market where the usual offer is a template, an open-ended bill, and a
number that stops answering."**

The competitor evidence for that last clause is in section 2.

**What the UVP is deliberately NOT:** "strategy-first", "premium", "we're
different". Each of those was tried and rejected earlier in this project because
none of them is observable by a buyer.

### Brand promise — FACT (three checkable sentences, already on the page)

1. **A written quote within 48 hours of your first message.**
2. **The price is fixed in writing, and the date it was fixed is on the page.**
3. **You own the domain, the hosting, the source and every account, in your name,
   on day one.**

**INFERENCE — why these three and not "quality":** a promise is only a promise if
the buyer can hold you to it. "Quality" cannot be checked. "48 hours" can. A
promise nobody can verify is decoration.

Real listings, checked November 2026. Prices are what the businesses publish.

| Business | Entry website | Mid | Higher | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Websys (Accra)** | GH₵2,500 (5 pages) | GH₵3,500 / GH₵5,500 | GH₵8,000 e-com; GH₵25,000+ custom app | SEO from GH₵2,500/mo |
| **KayTech Africa (Accra)** | GH₵3,500 (5 pages or fewer) | GH₵8,000–18,000 growth | Apps GH₵15,000; ERP GH₵25,000 | from GH₵2,000/mo |
| **BuiltFound** | **GH₵1,000** blog; GH₵1,600 business | GH₵2,500 e-com | — | GH₵600–1,500/yr maintenance |
| **YookuWeb (Accra)** | GH₵1,500 | GH₵2,500 business | GH₵3,500+ premium | 1–3 months free support |
| **Weis (diaspora/NGO)** | US$800 launch | — | — | — |

### What every one of them competes on — FACT

Cheaper, faster, more pages, SEO-included, revisions-included, a monthly
retainer. **The axis is price against page count against delivery time.** Nothing
else.

### The gaps — what they do not do

| Gap | Evidence | Who can own it |
|---|---|---|
| **Nobody publishes a fixed price that stays fixed** | Every table above is a *starting* price ("from", "+", "depends on scope") | SoftFire: "fixed in writing before work starts" |
| **Nobody says when the price was fixed** | Not one listing dates its quote | SoftFire: the date is on page one |
| **Nobody names what they will refuse** | Zero listings had an exclusion list | SoftFire: *"What I will not do"* + exclusions on page one |
| **Nobody shows the plan before the invoice** | All sell deliverables, none sell the thinking | SoftFire: six documents, published in full, for a real project |
| **Nobody hands over ownership as a headline** | Ownership is implied, never stated | SoftFire: "100% in your name" |

**INFERENCE — the position that follows:** *the one whose price does not move.*
That is a claim a buyer can test against five competitors in an afternoon, and it
is the only one on the list that the others cannot copy in a week, because
copying it means giving up the "from" price.

### The uncomfortable part — FACT

**SoftFire's own published prices are higher than four of the five listings
above.** Website GH₵1,500 against BuiltFound GH₵1,000 and YokuWeb GH₵2,500. The site
justifies this nowhere. Against a buyer comparing tables, the current page loses
on the only axis it does not address.

**FACT — repriced 2026-11-06, twice.** The .99 ladder was abandoned on
instruction and the Website was set to a **flat GH₵1,500, all-in, hosting
included**. See `../PRICING.md` for the full cost model.

| Studio | Entry website price |
|---|---|
| BuiltFound | GH₵1,000 |
| **SoftFire** | **GH₵1,500 — tied with YookuWeb** |
| YookuWeb | GH₵1,500 |
| Websys | GH₵2,500 |
| KayTech | GH₵3,500 |

**The old position is dead.** *"We cost more than everyone"* is now false and must
not appear anywhere. SoftFire is **second-cheapest of five**, level with a studio
that delivers in 5–7 days.

**INFERENCE — this is a different brand, not a discounted one.** At GH₵1,500 the
price no longer signals quality; it signals accessibility. The trust work must be
done entirely by published process, because the number is doing none of it. This
is a legitimate strategy — it is simply not the premium one, and the copy has to
stop implying otherwise.

**INFERENCE — the honest advantage that survives.** Every competitor price list in
this table **excludes** the domain and hosting. A GH₵1,500 that includes year-one
hosting is genuinely cheaper than a GH₵1,500 that does not. That is now the
strongest true claim available, and it is one sentence long.

**The strategic risk, stated plainly.** GH₵1,500 is about **30 hours** of work at
the local freelance floor — **2 to 4 days**, not 3 to 4 weeks. It is only viable
if the site is assembled from a system that already exists. If SoftFire designs
each site from nothing, GH₵1,500 pays **GH₵19–25/hour**, below the market floor,
for the hardest work. The price forces productisation. That is either the plan or
the trap.

**INFERENCE:** either the price is defended in one sentence on the page — *what
the difference buys* — or it is not a strategy,
it is a hope. The honest defence already exists in the product: a written plan, a
fixed price with a date, full ownership, and one named person who answers in
month six. None of that is on the price table.

## 3. Verbal and visual identity

### Name — FACT

**SoftFire.** Cap S, cap F, one word, no space. A trading name, **not a
registered company** (the footer says so).

**INFERENCE — why the name works:** it holds a contradiction in two syllables —
*soft* against *fire* — which is exactly the offer (patient process, immovable
result). It is easy to say in English and in Twi-accented English, and it is not a
category word ("Digital", "Solutions", "Hub"), so it does not dissolve into the
market.

### Tagline — FACT

**"The plan before the pixels."**

### Voice and personality — FACT (enforced by doc 04's brief, part 4)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chosen 2026-11-06** | *"Let's find out what your organisation actually needs."* — Caleb picked the consultative register over the clipped one |
| **Is** | plain, specific, unhedged; numbers instead of adjectives; **opens with a question more often than a claim** |
| **Is not** | "solutions", "revolutionise", "in today's fast-paced world", emoji, exclamation marks |
| **Refuses** | a promise of rankings, leads or growth; a date one person cannot hold |
| **The test** | every sentence must survive being read aloud to the person paying |

Worked example — same fact, two voices:

> No: "We craft bespoke digital experiences that elevate your brand."
>
> Yes: "Five pages, written for the buyer, built to load fast on a cheap phone.
> GH₵1,500. Fixed in writing before work starts."

**Tension to hold — INFERENCE.** The chosen voice is consultative; the prices are
published and fixed. Those pull against each other: a consultative studio normally
refuses to quote before a conversation. SoftFire does both by putting the *price*
on the page and the *diagnosis* in the conversation — the number is public, what
you need is not assumed. Write the question first, then the number, never the
other way round.

### Brand story — FACT (the only true version)

One developer. The first real project is **BiteKasa**, a food business built for a
family member who gave permission — *not a client, not a case study, not a
testimonial*. It is live, it takes orders on WhatsApp, and its seven URLs are
checked daily by a GitHub Action. That is the whole story: not "we have served 50
businesses", but "here is one that is real, and here is every document from it,
unfilled and then filled".

### Logo — FACT (locked, and now actually implemented)

Ordinary **S** and ordinary **F**, Inter Bold **700**, tracking **−0.03em**,
**typed, never drawn**. The fill is the only extraordinary thing.

**A defect found and fixed in this pass — FACT:** every mark was
`<text font-family="Inter">SF</text>` inside an SVG loaded through an `<img>`. An
SVG in an `<img>` is a separate document and cannot fetch the page's web fonts, so
the browser fell back to a system sans. Measured in Chromium, **three different S
shapes** appeared side by side. The mark was not the mark.

**Fixed:** `tools/build-marks.py` instantiates the shipped
`inter-latin-var.woff2` at `wght 700` with fontTools, applies −0.03em tracking,
and emits the glyphs as paths. The mark is now those two letters in every browser,
with no font request and no fallback.

| Tier | File | Use | Colours |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | logo-a-dark.svg | 64px cap and above | Gradient #B33A16 to #E85D04 to #F4A300, one ramp across the pair |
| **B** | logo-b.svg | 24px and above, screen | #F4A300 S, #E85D04 F on charcoal |
| **C** | logo-c.svg | under 24px, print, one colour | Cream on charcoal |
| **D** | logo-d-light.svg | light surfaces | Charcoal on cream |
| — | sf-mark.svg | the crawler field | Cream, transparent |

**Clear space:** 0.5x cap height on every side. **Minimum cap:** 64px (A), 24px
(B), 12px (C).

**UNKNOWN:** whether the mark survives a photocopy at 8mm, and whether it reads as
"SF" to someone who has not been told. Both need a human, not a measurement.

### Typography — FACT

**Inter** (variable, latin subset, 47.3 KB, self-hosted) and **JetBrains Mono**
(39.5 KB). Two families, both variable, both WOFF2, both font-display: swap, both
preloaded. The scale is 1.333 on a 16px base.

|---|---|---|---|
| Cream | #F2E9E4 | **15.75** | body |
| Gold | #F4A300 | **9.05** | accents, figures |
| Orange | #E85D04 | **5.38** | large text only |
| Muted | #A8968D | **6.65** | secondary |

Banned as text: cherry on charcoal, gold on orange (1.68), orange on cherry
(2.62), gold on cream (1.74), orange on cream (2.93).

### Imagery — FACT

No photography, no stock, no 3D blobs, no icons standing in for ideas. The only
recurring graphic is the SF tile, at bottle-cap size, on 8–12 vertical crawlers
moving in adjacent opposite directions. **Why:** it is the mark repeated at the
size a bottle cap prints at — a real thing a Ghanaian customer sees on a shelf,
not a decorative field.

## 4. Legal and digital protection — the weak part, stated honestly

### Trademarks — FACT (Trade Marks Act, 2004, Act 664)

| Step | Official fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Name search (optional but wise) | US$135 | Registrar searches for identical or similar marks |
| Application | US$240 | Trade Mark Form No. 2, one class per application, 4 representations |
| Certificate | US$240 | |
| **Total** | **about US$615** | Registry figures vary by source (US$510–615), so treat it as a range |
| Validity | 10 years | Renewable |
| Timeline | **about 2 years** | Published in the journal for 2 months for opposition |
| Non-use | 5 years | Registration vulnerable to cancellation |

**Class to file in:** Class 42 (design and development of computer software;
website design). **FACT:** registration covers Ghana only.

**UNKNOWN / must decide:** whether to file at all. US$615 against a business that
has not yet charged a stranger US$615 is a real trade-off, and only Caleb can make
it. The cheap intermediate step is the **US$135 search** — knowing whether
"SoftFire" is already someone's mark in Class 42 costs a sixth of filing and
answers the question that matters most: *can this name be mine?*

### Domain names — FACT, and it contradicts what this project assumed

This project's earlier notes said `softfire.dev` "does not exist". **That is
wrong.** Measured:

| Domain | DNS | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| softfire.dev | resolves, 104.21.85.42 and 172.67.202.70 (Cloudflare) | **registered and hosted** |
| softfire.com | resolves, 75.126.104.234 | **registered and hosted** |
| softfire.com.gh | NXDOMAIN | **appears available** |
| softfire.gh | NXDOMAIN | **appears available** |
| softfire.site, softfire.app | NXDOMAIN | appear available |

**What this means:** the .com and the .dev belong to someone else. A Ghanaian
customer typing softfire.com reaches a stranger. `softfire.com.gh` — the one that
reads as a local business — looks free.

**DECIDED 2026-11-06:** **stay on `hotmoon123.github.io/softfire/` for now.** Caleb's
call: no domain spend until money comes in. That is consistent with the vision —
the first paid project matters more than the URL — but it has one consequence
worth stating so it is a decision and not an accident:

**INFERENCE — the cost of waiting.** A church or school buyer who wants to check
SoftFire exists will read `github.io` as *hobby*, not *studio*. It does not read
as dishonest; it reads as unfinished. The price card and the document pack are
doing all the trust work, and the URL is doing none. That is survivable at
GH₵1,500 for a site — nobody expects a hobbyist to own a `.com.gh` — and stops
being survivable for a GH₵14,999 Portal, where a buyer is handing a stranger five
figures and will look for reasons not to.

**What would trigger the switch:** the first paid project. Register
`softfire.com.gh` at that point, not before, and not with a card that is not
already being paid off. **Do not** build the brand on .dev — it belongs to someone
else.

**UNKNOWN:** the price of .com.gh (Ghana's registry pricing is not published
consistently and I will not guess it), and whether the owner of softfire.dev would
sell.

### Social handles

**UNKNOWN.** No Instagram exists yet (stated). The earlier instruction was to
reference `@msdigitalsunshine` as the model for how an Instagram presence is run —
**not** as a SoftFire handle. Handle availability for `@softfire` on Instagram,
TikTok and Facebook is **unverified** and must be checked before the name is
printed on anything.

### Business registration — FACT

Currently a **trading name on a personal MoMo number**, not a registered business.
The site footer states this rather than hiding it. **INFERENCE:** this is the
single highest-value legal step, above the trademark, because a registered
business name is what lets a church or school put SoftFire on a purchase order at
all.

## 5. Customer experience and growth

### Consistency across touchpoints — FACT (checked, not asserted)

| Touchpoint | What it is |
|---|---|
| Website | One page, ten sections, no third-party request, 177 KB first load |
| WhatsApp | 0570 572 221 — the primary CTA on every screen; the brief composes the first message |
| Email | kojocbentum@gmail.com |
| Phone | 0597 937 024 (second number) |
| GitHub | github.com/hotmoon123 — the work is public, including this repo |
| Documents | Six, published in full, for a real project |

**The consistency test that matters:** the voice on the page, in the six
documents, and in a WhatsApp reply must be the same voice. All three currently
share one rule — *name the thing, give the number, do not hedge.*

### Customer feedback and trust — UNKNOWN, and this is the biggest gap

There are **no reviews, no testimonials and no case results**, because there are no
clients yet. BiteKasa is a family member's business, built with permission —
**never a client, never a testimonial.** The site says this plainly and it is the
right call: one invented five-star review would poison every other checkable claim
on the page.

**CONFIRMED 2026-11-06.** Asked directly whether a stranger had ever paid for
this work, Caleb answered **no** — only BiteKasa, for family. So this is not a
gap that closes next week. Any brand written to assume a first testimonial is
arriving soon is written for a business that does not exist yet.

**What to do instead, in order:**

1. **Ask BiteKasa's owner for one sentence** she would say unprompted — her words,
   her name, her permission. That is a real quote, not a testimonial, and it is
   worth more than five invented ones.
2. **Publish the first three enquiries** — even the ones that went nowhere, with
   permission and no names. "Three people asked; one bought" is credible.
3. **Keep the 48-hour promise in public and count it.** "Every quote sent within 48
   hours since [date]: 11 of 11" is a trust asset nobody else in the table above
   can print.

**The floor, stated plainly — INFERENCE.** With zero paying clients, the only
proof available is *process* proof: published documents, a verifier anyone can
run, a price that does not move, and a named person. SoftFire should sell the
process loudly and the track record not at all. The first paid project is worth
more to this brand than any redesign — and it should be documented to the same
standard as BiteKasa.
