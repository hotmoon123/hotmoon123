## 2. Profile setup (do first, 20 minutes)

**Status of the profile on 5 Nov 2026:** photo set (logo), name added, **bio missing**, 1 link missing — Instagram shows “3 of 4 complete”.

**Name field** (searchable, **30-character limit**) — copy this:
```
BiteKasa Samosas & Rolls Accra
```
Exactly 30 characters. Instagram rejects anything longer.

The requested wording — `BiteKasa | Spring rolls & Samosas Accra` — is **39 characters and will not fit**. Brand name, both product words and the city need 31 letters before separators, so one element has to give. Options that fit:

| Name field | Chars | What you keep | What you lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `BiteKasa Samosas & Rolls Accra` | 30 | Brand, both products, city | “Spring” spelled out in full |
| `BiteKasa \| Spring Rolls Accra` | 29 | Brand, “Spring Rolls”, city | “Samosas” |
| `Spring Rolls & Samosas \| Accra` | 30 | Both products, city | Brand in the name field (still visible as `@bitekasa`) |

Recommendation: the first. Search on Instagram matches the name field, so it should read like what a customer would type — and a customer in Accra types “samosa”, not “BiteKasa”. The username already carries the brand.

#### Why not put the owner's personal name in the name field?

It is a real strategy choice, not a technical one. The name field has one job and only 30 characters, and it can do one of three things:

| It can… | Works when | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Reinforce the brand | Always, mildly | Uses characters a searcher would have typed anyway |
| Carry search keywords | Always — this is how discovery actually happens | No brand reinforcement |
| Carry a person's name | **Only if customers already know that person's name** | Brand and category both lose space |

BiteKasa is currently found through the flyer, the QR, WhatsApp forwards and word of mouth — every one of those says **BiteKasa**, not a person's name. A name field reading something else breaks that chain at the exact moment someone searches for what they just saw on paper.

Put the owner's name in the name field only if **that name is how customers already refer to the business** — “Auntie Ama's samosas” rather than “BiteKasa samosas”. Founder-led food brands do succeed this way, but the recognition has to exist first; it cannot be installed by the profile.

If it does exist, a name-first field fits: `Ama's Samosas & Rolls | Accra` (29 characters). If it does not, use `BiteKasa Samosas & Rolls Accra` and let the personal story live in the captions, the Reels and the WhatsApp replies, where it builds warmth without spending the characters.

## 5. WhatsApp conversational funnel

**Stage 1 — Instagram Story** → link sticker or “Message us” sticker (if the account has the feature) + sticker text “Packs in bio”.
**Stage 2 — DM auto-reply (saved reply):**
```
Thanks for messaging BiteKasa. Which pack — Silver, Golden or Premium —
and which area are you in? We'll confirm availability and price.
```
**Stage 3 — WhatsApp** → move the conversation across with a prefilled message:
`https://wa.me/233595638696?text=Hi%20BiteKasa%2C%20I%27d%20like%20to%20order%20the%20...%20pack`
**Stage 4 — close** → confirm pack, quantity, area, time; then send payment/collection instructions.
**Stage 5 — after** → one follow-up message 2–3 days later; ask for a photo or a line about the food **with permission**, and only then use it as proof.

## 6. Community management and reputation

- **Reply target:** within 2 hours during business hours, same day otherwise.
- **Tone:** plain, warm, specific. No emoji walls, no all-caps.
- **Never argue publicly.** Take disputes to DM, then to a call.
- **Allergens and ingredients:** answer only with owner-confirmed facts; otherwise “let me confirm and come back to you”.
- **Crisis case — a wrong price or wrong count is posted:** correct it in the same thread within the hour, state the correct figure, honour the posted price for anyone who already enquired, and fix the source file. Do not delete and repost silently.

## 8. Creators and paid — deliberately later

- **Now:** no creator spend. With no baseline, a paid test cannot be evaluated.
- **When there is a baseline** (roughly four weeks of enquiry numbers): choose micro-creators in Accra on **audience fit and authenticity**, not follower count; brief them with the actual pack facts; require **#ad / #sponsored** disclosure in the first two lines and in the first three seconds of video; give them the real product to photograph, not the illustrative renders.
- **Never:** buy followers or engagement, use fake reviews, or imply a partnership that does not exist.

---

## 9. What this playbook deliberately does not claim

- Follower counts, reach, engagement or sales — no data exists yet.
- “Viral”, “trending”, “best in Accra”.
- That posting this calendar produces orders. It produces a consistent presence; the measurement table is how you find out what works.

### The honest diagnosis

At three followers and roughly eleven views on the best post, the account is **not being distributed** — this is a cold start, not a content-quality problem. Two posts in a month is not a presence, and static “price poster” images almost never travel beyond existing followers. Posting a third poster will produce the same result.

Three things, in this order, change that:

1. **Make the profile convertible first.** A visitor arriving from a flyer or a WhatsApp forward currently has no bio, no prices and no link. Fix that in minutes — the copy above.
2. **Get the first 50 followers from people who already know BiteKasa.** Existing customers, the church network, family, the WhatsApp contacts who have ordered before. That is what makes the follow graph real. Sharing the profile link in WhatsApp status is the highest-yield action available this week.
3. **Post video, not posters.** Reels are the only organic surface that shows content to non-followers. Kitchen clips — folding, frying, packing — at 3 posts a week for four weeks. The pack/price information belongs in the caption and carousel, not as the hook.

### What to do this week

| Day | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Today | Paste name, bio and link; add contact buttons | Converts existing traffic |
| Today | Share the profile link in WhatsApp status + to the last 20 people who ordered | First real follower base |
| Today | Archive the two image posts described below | Removes a credibility risk |
| Day 2–3 | Film one 15-second clip: folding or frying, no talking | First Reel; test whether non-followers appear |
| Day 4 | Post it with the “How to order” caption | Measures whether distribution moves at all |
| Day 5–7 | Two more clips; reply to every comment and DM same day | Consistency + response signal |
| Day 7 | Record the six numbers again in the table in section 7 | First real comparison point |

**Target for 30 days — deliberately modest:** 50 followers, at least one post with more views than followers, and 10 tracked enquiries. Not “viral”. If the enquiry count is zero after four weeks of consistent video, the problem is the offer or the audience, not the posting schedule — and that is worth knowing.

### Two posts that should come down

Both visible posts use an image of a **generated-looking woman holding the product**. She is not a customer, not the owner, and not a staff member. Presenting her beside BiteKasa packs implies an endorsement or a real customer story that does not exist — a consumer-protection risk, and against Instagram's policy on AI-generated content when it is not disclosed.

They also carry copy that was already rejected for the flyer: **“MORE BITES. MORE COKE. MORE VALUE.”**

Recommendation: archive both, replace with real photography or video of actual BiteKasa products. If any generated image is kept anywhere, disclose it in the caption and in Instagram's AI-generated content flag. Never caption a generated image as “our customer”.
